# Treatment: "The Fake Bank Text" (Mid-century Cartoon, 5:00, 1080x1920)

Style: 50s Mid-century Cartoon (scene style, "safety / rules card" grammar). Language: English. Voice: Kokoro, calm classroom narrator. Music: original cool-jazz combo. No existing brands: the bank is a fictional "Maple Trust".

## Three candidate structures
1. **Rules card only.** Seven numbered rules, one held figure and a medallion each. Clear, but flat as a five-minute watch; nothing is at stake.
2. **One victim's journey.** Walter gets the text, follows it step by step, loses money, the end. Dramatic, but it teaches how to be scammed, not how to stop it, and ends on a downer.
3. **Anatomy, then rewind.** Part I follows one fake text through five stages (the bait, the panic, the link, the copycat page, the code). A "rewind" turns the numbers backwards. Part II replays the same five moments, each time with the right move (do / don't split). Part III: "already clicked?" four steps, then a checklist that ticks itself.

**Chosen: 3.** It shows how the scam works (the topic) and, by the replay, lets the character do the thing right the second time (DIRECTOR §3 echo ending). The five stages are mirrored one-to-one by the five defences, so the viewer remembers one pattern.

## Logline and arc
A text that looks like it is from your bank walks through five tricks to take your money; rewound, the same five moments each have a calm counter-move.
Setup (hook: the text, 0:00) → turn (the rewind, 2:30, preceded by a near-silence) → ending (checklist ticks itself; dead-still frame with the one rule, 4:55).

## Benchmark
Learn: 1950s classroom/safety films: one sentence per picture, numbered cards, direct address; UPA-era colour planes and held bodies. Do not take: any character, lettering, music or title from those works.

## Shot list and camera
At least five moves: slow push (phone / page), iris through the numbered medallion (every stage), round call-out with leader line (the lookalike address, the code), tilt with pointing finger (the one thing to look at), colour-block wipe (rule changes), split frame (do / don't), pull-back (stage recap), dead-still 3 s (end). Key subjects ≥ 1/3 frame height and clear of the caption band at the bottom.

## Cue map and sound
- Tempo 100 bpm, Part I in a minor feel (vibraphone sparse, brushes, walking pizzicato bass, muted trumpet or flute for the tension), Part II brighter (major vibraphone chords, one chord per rule, flute lead). Each stage has its own vibraphone chord; they return in the final checklist, one per item.
- Silences: before the first text (phone buzz only), and 2:24 to 2:28 everything cut except a room tone and the clock; the first sound afterwards is the medallion stamp. A second near-silence before "Keep the code".
- Foley: phone buzz, tap, paper swish for wipes, rubber-stamp thump for medallions, slide whistle for arrows, clock tick, click, keyboard typing, a rewind tape whirr, rising pips for ticks.
- Sound as transition: next scene's brush pattern enters a beat before the wipe (J-cut); the last vibraphone note rings over the cut (L-cut).
- Mix: voice on top, music ducked about 8 dB, foley unducked, −14 LUFS.

## Safety and accuracy
No instructions that help a scammer: the film names the signs and the defences, not how to build the page. Advice stays general and region-neutral: official app or number on your card, never share one-time codes, report to the bank and the national fraud authority. No statistics are quoted. Bank name, phone number and URL are fictional.
