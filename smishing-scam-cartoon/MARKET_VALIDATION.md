# Is "The Second Look" a real opportunity? Market validation

Date: 17 Nov 2026. **Method and limits:** web searches only. I could not open YouTube, Reddit, Google Trends, vidIQ or Social Blade from this environment. So I have **good evidence of demand** and **thin evidence about competition and virality**. I did not find proof that the niche is empty, and you should not assume it is.

## Verdict
| Question | Answer | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Is there real, lasting demand? | **Yes, strong.** | High |
| Is there little competition? | **Partly.** The topic is crowded; the specific format (calm animated lesson for ordinary people) looked open in my searches, but I could not check YouTube. | Low to medium |
| Can it go viral? | **Possible, but not the likeliest outcome.** Shareable and evergreen, but a safety lesson rarely spreads like entertainment. Expect slow, compounding growth, not a spike. | Low |
| Can it make money? | **Yes, mainly outside AdSense**, and slowly. Shorts RPM is low. | Medium |

Recommendation: **proceed as a 60 to 90 day test, not a bet.** Make 10 Shorts and 3 lessons, and use the thresholds at the end to decide.

## 1. Demand (evidence)
- The FTC reports about **$3.5 billion** lost to imposter scams in 2025, the top fraud category for the fifth year; business impersonation was about $1 billion and the highest losses involved criminals pretending to be a bank. [FTC, June 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/06/ftc-data-show-people-reported-losing-3-point-5-billion-imposter-scams-2025), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/26/imposter-scams-led-fraud-reports-to-ftc-in-2025-3point5-billion-losses.html)
- Text scams: about **$470 million** in 2024, up from $330 million in 2022, with bank impersonation the most reported text scam in 2022. (FTC; I found no 2025 text-only total.)
- Local news and consumer sites run "your bank account is locked" stories repeatedly, which signals steady audience interest. [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/money/scams-fraud/online-banking-fraud/), [Aura](https://aura.com/learn/fake-bank-text-messages)
- Reports of impersonation scams that took large sums from older adults rose more than fourfold, which keeps the "protect my parents" angle alive. [FTC, Aug 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/08/ftc-data-show-more-four-fold-increase-reports-impersonation-scammers-stealing-tens-even-hundreds)
- AI and deepfake-enabled scams are expected to keep rising. [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2025/11/04/youtube-video-scams-cybercrime/)

**Demand gap:** no search-volume numbers. Check "is this text from my bank real", "bank text scam" and "i clicked a scam link what do i do" in Google Trends and YouTube autocomplete.

## 2. Competition (evidence)
- **Big creators are scam-baiters and investigators**, with millions of subscribers: Jim Browning, Kitboga, Scammer Payback and others. Their audience wants entertainment and revenge, not lessons. [About Fraud](https://www.about-fraud.com/youtube-fraud-channels/), [Trustfull](https://trustfull.com/articles/top-12-youtube-channels-about-fraud-scams-financial-crime)
- **Institutions** (FTC, AARP, NCOA, banks, local TV) own the written answer to "what do I do", but they are not good at short, shareable, well-designed video.
- I **did not find a large animated channel** doing calm lessons on fake bank texts. That could mean a gap, or that my search missed it. **I cannot confirm it.**
- "Scam School" is a University of Notre Dame series, not a channel competitor. [ND](https://oit.nd.edu/initiatives/scam-school/)
- A site called ClearSight uses a similar "Pause. Question. Verify." framework and links to a YouTube channel; check it by hand. (clearsighthq.pages.dev)

**What this means:** you will not beat the baiters on entertainment; you win on **calm clarity and design**. The risk is that bigger channels or institutions copy the format.

## 3. Virality (honest view)
- Search-driven evergreen content compounds well: "fake bank text" is asked every time a new wave arrives.
- Shorts RPM is tiny (about 3 to 14% of long-form RPM per the AIR data in the earlier report). Money comes from long videos, affiliate links, sponsors and B2B training.
- A scam-awareness Short has an unusual advantage: people **send it to a parent**. Design every video for that share.
- I found **no view-count benchmarks** for animated scam-awareness Shorts. One corporate case reported strong watch time for a scam-scenario video, but that is not comparable.

## 4. Audience (see `audience.md`)
Primary: the worried helper (adult children), not the elderly viewer. Evidence: seniors mostly rely on TV, newspapers and mail for scam alerts (cited by a scam-prevention group; University of Michigan). So the video must work as something one person shows or sends to another.

## 5. GEO: being cited by AI answer engines
**What the research says** (secondary sources, treat as directional): Reddit, YouTube, Wikipedia and LinkedIn are among the most cited sources; Google's AI features lean on YouTube (one study: 36% of AI Overview sources); government and education sites make up a modest share. [Contently](https://contently.com/2026/04/29/top-sources-llms-cite/), [Frase](https://www.frase.io/blog/which-ai-engines-cite-which-sources). I found **no study of scam or phishing queries specifically**, so run your own audit.

**Plan (authentic only; no astroturfing, no fake reviews):**
1. **Prompt audit first (week 0, then monthly).** In fresh sessions on ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini, ask the 20 prompts below, and record whether any YouTube video or channel is cited and which sources win. Do not invent a "share of voice" number.
2. **Be retrievable:** YouTube titles with the exact question phrase, captions, a chapter list, and a transcript on a simple page. Check that robots.txt does not block AI crawlers on any site you create.
3. **Be extractable:** each video page and transcript starts with a one-sentence answer ("A real bank will never ask you to read out a one-time code."), uses question headings, a short FAQ, and cites official sources (your national fraud authority, FTC, your bank). Put dates on pages and refresh them.
4. **Earn mentions honestly:** answer questions in communities (for example r/Scams) with real help first and your video only where it truly answers the question and the rules allow it. Disclose that you made it.
5. **Entity clarity:** one sentence everywhere: "The Second Look makes calm animated lessons that help everyday people spot fake bank texts and phishing." Same name, description and links across YouTube, a website and social profiles.

**20 audit prompts:** how to tell if a bank text is real | is this text from my bank a scam | bank text says account locked what do I do | I clicked a link in a scam text what now | can a bank ask for my one-time code | why do scam texts say act now | how do scammers copy bank websites | what is smishing | how to protect my parents from scam texts | best videos explaining phishing | animated phishing explained | how to report a scam text | passkey vs password safety | what is two-step verification | fake delivery text scam | are links in bank texts safe | how to check a link before opening | what to do after sharing a code | how to teach seniors about phishing | best YouTube channels for online safety.

## 6. Risks
- **Policy:** YouTube judges "inauthentic content" at channel level. A repeated template with a synthetic voice can be flagged. Mitigate with real scripts, varied formats, and your own on-camera or recorded voice later.
- **Accuracy and liability:** one wrong tip hurts trust. Cite official sources; no legal or financial advice.
- **Misuse:** teach recognition, not method.
- **Audience mismatch:** the people at risk are not mostly on YouTube; growth depends on the helper segment.

## 7. Go / no-go test (60 to 90 days)
Publish 10 Shorts and 3 lessons, then judge:
- At least 2 videos with **5x or more views than the channel median**, or 1 video above 50,000 views.
- Shorts viewed-vs-swiped rate above 70%, and long-video average view duration above 40%.
- Subscribers per 1,000 views of at least 5.
- At least 3 real comments or messages like "I sent this to my mum".
- In the prompt audit, at least one of your videos cited for some query after 8 weeks.
If none of these happen, change the angle (for example a series made for parents to watch with their children) before investing more.

## 8. What to check by hand (I could not)
1. YouTube search for the 10 phrases in `audience.md`: note the top 10 results, channel size, upload dates and views.
2. vidIQ or Social Blade: find channels under 50,000 subscribers with a video above 100,000 views (a strong sign of an open niche).
3. Google Trends over 5 years for "bank text scam", "smishing", "fake text".
4. Reddit r/Scams: how often "is this text real" is asked.
