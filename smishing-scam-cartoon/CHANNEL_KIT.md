## Settings that help discovery (all legitimate; none of this is a trick)
1. **Consistent format:** one repeating series ("Lesson N: ..."), one look, one colour scheme. Viewers and YouTube recognise the series.
2. **Home page:** trailer for people who haven't subscribed (the 5-minute film or a 30 s cut), "Start here" playlist first, then the series.
3. **Playlists:** "Start here", "Fake texts and bank messages", "Codes and passwords", "If you already clicked", "Shorts".
4. **Shorts as the front door:** cut the film into 5 Shorts (one per answer) and end each with "Full lesson on the channel". Link each Short to the full video.
5. **Search (YouTube SEO):** put the viewer's phrase in the title and the first 2 lines of the description ("fake bank text", "how to spot a fake text"). Keep tags few and accurate. Add captions and chapters.
6. **AI answer engines (GEO):** keep the channel name, description and website text identical across platforms. Write one plain sentence that defines what each video teaches. Add a transcript on a simple web page with a "How it works" section and a short FAQ ("Can a bank ask me for my one-time code? No."). Cite official sources (your national fraud authority, your bank) on the page. Answer engines prefer clear, sourced, consistent statements.
7. **Languages:** turn on multi-language audio or auto-dubbing for the main languages you care about, and translate the title and description. Check whether Hebrew is supported.
8. **Community:** reply to comments in the first hour, use end screens to the next lesson in the series, and make a Community post for each new video.
9. **Cadence:** one 5-minute lesson and 3 to 5 Shorts a week is realistic. Do not mass-produce near-identical videos: YouTube's current rule on inauthentic content applies to the whole channel.
